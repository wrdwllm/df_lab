Project by: Alexander Holland, Stanislav Roudavski, Phillip Gibbons

> Grassy box gum woodlands once covered millions of square kilometres in southeastern Australia, but today [less than 5% remains](https://www.nespthreatenedspecies.edu.au/projects/conservation-of-box-gum-grassy-woodlands-and-the-threatened-species-within-them). The loss of [large old trees](https://theconversation.com/the-end-of-big-trees-11217) has been a crisis for the many species of birds and other animals that depend on them for habitat.
>
> Replacing this habitat is not easy. There is no quick way to create a centuries-old tree.
>
> One thing we can do is make artificial structures that mimic the features of large old trees in degraded environments where trees cannot live or are too young and small. We have been working with the Australian Capital Territory Parks and Conservation Service to do just this in the Molonglo region of Canberra.

[The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/what-makes-a-good-tree-we-used-ai-to-ask-birds-233281)

#### Stakeholders
The approach is relevant for [[tags/stakeholder/bird|birds]] in general, but species have unique requirements.

For example, the [[../Stakeholders/Superb fairywren|Superb fairywren]] requires on mid-storey shrubs and ground cover under 2m high.

 The majority of non-human species also prefer a level of protection from noise, light, predators, disruption, pollution, and weather. These factors should be considered when considering siting.
